Constitutional Monetary Governance Protocol  

# Monetary Architecture & Banking Protocol

## Article I: Institutional Framework
**1.1. Abolition of Central Banking.** The Central Bank is hereby abolished and supplanted by the **Currency Directorate** (a Modified Currency Board). The Directorate operates under a strict constitutional mandate, devoid of discretionary monetary policy powers.

**1.2. The Denationalization of Money.** In accordance with the Hayekian principle, the state authorizes the issuance of **parallel currencies** by licensed commercial banks. Banks are permitted to compete through credit rates, deposit yields, and the quality of banking secrecy.

**1.3. Currency Stabilization.** To ensure real-world value, every banking currency must be backed by a **Currency Basket**. The basket composition is restricted to:
*   **USD:** $\le 25\%$
*   **EUR:** $\le 25\%$
*   **Any other single currency:** $\le 20\%$

## Article II: The Dynamic Monetary Cap
**2.1. The Growth Algorithm.** To balance stability with economic expansion, the Directorate enforces a non-discretionary **Dynamic Limit** on the total volume of banking money per institution.
$$\text{Limit}_{t+1} = \text{Limit}_t \times (1 + 0.0001)$$
*   **Initial Base:** 10 billion units per bank.
*   **Daily Growth Rate:** $+0.01\%$ (compounded daily).

**2.2. Anti-Monopoly Constraint.** The dynamic cap serves as a systemic circuit-breaker. By limiting the maximum scale of any single institution, the protocol prevents the emergence of "Too Big to Fail" entities and ensures that competitive growth is qualitative rather than merely quantitative.

**2.3. Market Diversity.** To maintain a healthy ecosystem, a minimum of **20 independent banking institutions** must operate within the jurisdiction to prevent oligopolistic collusion.

## Article III: Resource Restitution Mechanism (The Seigniorage Levy)
**3.1. Mandatory Penalty.** Any institution exceeding its prescribed Dynamic Limit is subject to an immediate and non-negotiable **Resource Restitution Levy**.

**3.2. Composition of the Levy.** The penalty shall be paid in physical precious and noble metals at a rate of **one (1) metric ton per day** until the limit is restored. To prevent market distortion, the levy must be diversified:
*   **Per-Element Minimum:** $10\text{ kg}$ (ensures mandatory diversification).
*   **Per-Element Maximum:** $100\text{ kg}$ (prevents single-resource saturation).
*   **Eligible Assets:** Gold, Platinum-group metals (PGM), Titanium, Cobalt, Lithium, and approved Rare Earth Elements (REE).

**3.3. R&D Appropriation.** All resources collected via the Restitution Levy are strictly prohibited from entering the general budget. They shall be allocated directly to **State R&D (Research & Development)** and strategic technological initiatives.

## Article IV: Systemic Resilience & Legal Audit
**4.1. Interbank Stability Fund.** All systemic risks shall be managed via a **Mutual Interbank Rescue Fund**. Taxpayer-funded bailouts are constitutionally prohibited. Banks are collectively responsible for the solvency of the system.

**4.2. Algorithmic Audit.** The Currency Directorate functions as a technical auditor. The determination of a limit breach is a binary mathematical fact, removing all discretionary power from the regulator.

**4.3. Judicial Recourse.** Any discrepancy in the Directorate’s audit is subject to immediate review by the **Ministry of Justice** and the **Constitutional Court**. The transparency of the monetary ledger ensures that corruption within the Directorate is a self-reporting crime, detectable by any competing bank.
